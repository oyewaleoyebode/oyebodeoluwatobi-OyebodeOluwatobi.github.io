## Descriptions & Datasets

### Encrypted Speaker Verification

This ongoing project investigates the progressive use of homomorphic encryption in protected speaker verification. It examines how encrypting different components of the verification process affects authentication reliability, numerical stability, and computational efficiency. The objective is to determine the practical extent to which speaker verification can be performed in the encrypted domain while preserving dependable verification decisions.

### Privacy Analysis of Protected Speaker Verification

This ongoing project examines the privacy boundaries of protected and encrypted speaker-verification systems. It investigates whether sensitive information may remain accessible through protected representations and verification outputs and considers additional privacy mechanisms for reducing potential leakage while maintaining authentication utility.

### Cancellable Speaker Credentials under Template Exposure

This project addresses the long-term security risks created when stored voice-biometric templates are exposed. Unlike passwords, a person’s biometric characteristics cannot simply be changed after compromise. We developed a cancellable speaker-credential framework that transforms speaker representations into protected credentials before storage and verification. The framework is designed to support revocability, allowing an exposed credential to be cancelled and replaced; unlinkability, reducing the ability to connect credentials issued for different applications; and verification utility, preserving the information required for reliable authentication. The system was evaluated under a template-exposure threat model to determine whether protected speaker credentials could resist unauthorized reuse and cross-application linkage while maintaining speaker-verification performance. This work establishes cancellable transformation as an important protection layer for secure voice-biometric authentication.

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/ACCESS.2026.3710210)

### Adaptive Speaker Verification under Degradation

This project investigates how acoustic degradation affects the reliability and security of speaker-verification systems. Voice samples were progressively degraded to represent changes caused by noise, recording devices, communication channels, and poor acoustic conditions. As degradation increased, STOI decreased from approximately 0.692 to 0.279 and PESQ decreased from approximately 2.867 to 1.197. At a fixed verification threshold of 0.7, accuracy decreased from 94% under the baseline condition to 72%, 12%, 4%, and ultimately 0% across increasingly severe degradation levels. Although the false-acceptance rate remained at 0%, legitimate speakers were increasingly rejected, demonstrating that a fixed threshold can become operationally unreliable. We developed a drift-aware security framework that uses changes in speech quality and verification behaviour to support adaptive responses such as threshold adjustment, additional liveness verification, fallback authentication, or rejection of unusable speech samples.

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/AAIML67890.2026.11498091)

### Speaker Identification for Low-End Devices

This project develops a secure voice-biometric authentication system for mobile-banking users who rely on low-end devices without dependable fingerprint, facial-recognition, or other dedicated biometric sensors. The system combines Whisper and ECAPA-TDNN representations to capture complementary speech and speaker information and applies dimensionality reduction and machine-learning methods for speaker identification. It was evaluated using recordings collected from 50 speakers across multiple devices, communication conditions, and indoor and outdoor environments. The system achieved an identification accuracy of 99.59% and an F1-score of approximately 99.60%. A liveness-detection component based on Logistic Regression and Random Forest was also incorporated to strengthen protection against non-live voice samples, achieving 99.96% cross-validation accuracy and 100% test accuracy. The results demonstrate the potential of voice biometrics as an accessible additional authentication mechanism for mobile-banking environments involving resource-constrained devices.

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/PCDS65695.2025.00020)  
[Code](https://github.com/oyewaleoyebode/Speaker-Identification-for-Low-End-Devices-A-Secure-Voice-Biometric-Solution-for-Mobile-Banking)

### Optimizing Voice Biometric Verification in Banking

This project investigates how machine-learning and speech-processing methods can improve speaker identification across different mobile devices and recording environments. Voice-biometric systems can experience substantial performance changes because of differences in microphone hardware, device quality, background noise, and communication channels. We developed a processing pipeline involving speech preprocessing, enhancement, feature extraction, dimensionality reduction, and machine-learning-based speaker classification. Voice recordings collected across different devices and acoustic environments were used to determine whether speaker identity could be recognised reliably despite real-world variability. The findings established the technical foundation for subsequent work on speaker identification for low-end devices, degradation-aware authentication, cancellable speaker credentials, and privacy-preserving encrypted verification.

[Paper](https://doi.org/10.1109/APCC62576.2024.10768085)  
[Code](https://github.com/oyewaleoyebode/Optimizing-Voice-Biometric-Verification-in-Banking-with-Machine-Learning-for-Speaker-Identification)

### SecureVoice50

SecureVoice50 is a multi-device and multi-environment speech dataset developed for research on robust and secure speaker verification. The dataset contains recordings from 50 speakers—20 female and 30 male participants between 21 and 38 years old—from diverse linguistic and cultural backgrounds across Africa, Asia, and Europe. Approximately one hour of speech was collected from each participant using low-end and conventional mobile devices, in-call channels, and an external microphone. Recordings were produced under indoor and outdoor conditions at sampling rates between 8 kHz and 16 kHz to capture realistic variations caused by microphones, device hardware, communication channels, and environmental noise. The dataset supports research on speaker identification, speaker verification, mobile-banking authentication, speech degradation, liveness detection, biometric-template protection, and privacy-preserving authentication.

### Pilot Speech Corpus for Device and Environmental Variability

This pilot corpus was developed to study how recording devices and acoustic environments affect speaker-identification and verification systems. It contains 480 recordings from 12 participants collected using a Samsung A04s, OnePlus Nord, iPhone 15 Pro, and USB condenser microphone under indoor and outdoor conditions. The corpus captures cross-device and cross-environment variability that can alter speech quality, speaker representations, and authentication performance. It supports research on speech enhancement, robust speaker recognition, mobile voice authentication, and the evaluation of biometric systems under realistic operating conditions. Findings from this pilot study informed the design and collection of the larger SecureVoice50 dataset.
