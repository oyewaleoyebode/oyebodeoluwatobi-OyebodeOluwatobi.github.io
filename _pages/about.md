## Hi There 👋

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Information Science and Engineering at the **Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST), Japan**.

My research lies at the intersection of **cybersecurity, secure voice biometrics, and privacy-preserving authentication**. I develop secure and deployable speaker-authentication systems that protect sensitive biometric information while preserving verification reliability and decision integrity. My work particularly focuses on biometric template protection, cancellable speaker credentials, encrypted verification, privacy-leakage analysis, and cyber resilience.

My current doctoral research investigates **privacy-preserving speaker verification**. In particular, I examine whether protected speaker representations retain their verification structure, reliability, and decision integrity after cancellable transformation and homomorphic encryption. My ongoing work includes CKKS-based encrypted similarity computation, progressive encryption of the verification process, cross-backend verification-integrity analysis, privacy-leakage evaluation, and differential privacy.

I developed **cancellable speaker credentials** to address the security risks created when stored voice-biometric templates are exposed. The approach transforms speaker representations into revocable and unlinkable credentials while preserving the information required for speaker verification. This work was published in *IEEE Access* in 2026.

I also developed a **drift-aware security framework for speaker verification under acoustic degradation**. This research investigates how background noise, device differences, and communication-channel degradation affect verification performance and authentication reliability. The work was presented at AAIML 2026.

During my master’s degree at NAIST, I worked on secure voice-biometric systems for mobile banking and resource-constrained devices. I developed speaker-identification and verification pipelines using speech enhancement, machine learning, deep speaker embeddings, and liveness detection. This research resulted in publications at IEEE APCC 2024 and IEEE PCDS 2025.

I created **SecureVoice50**, a multi-device and multi-environment speech dataset containing recordings from 50 speakers. I also developed a pilot speech corpus containing 480 recordings from 12 participants across different recording devices and environments. These datasets support reproducible research on device variability, acoustic degradation, speaker identification, and secure speaker verification.

I will undertake a research internship at **Toshiba Corporation** focusing on **cyber resilience for infrastructure systems**. This work will examine cybersecurity and resilience challenges associated with infrastructure systems, including threat analysis, system reliability, and security-aware evaluation.

Before beginning my graduate studies, I worked in information technology and network administration. My experience included managing organizational IT infrastructure, configuring networks, implementing firewalls, VPNs, and access controls, supporting system continuity, and delivering technical training. This professional background provided the practical foundation for my current research in cybersecurity and secure authentication.

My master’s studies at NAIST were supported by the **Japan Student Services Organization (JASSO) Scholarship**, for which I am grateful.
