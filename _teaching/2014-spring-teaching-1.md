## Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST)

### Graduate Research Assistant — Doctoral Research

**October 2025 – Present**  
Ikoma, Nara, Japan

Conduct research on **privacy-preserving speaker authentication** for secure digital identity systems. My work examines how speaker representations can be protected during storage and verification without compromising authentication reliability or decision integrity.

- Designed cancellable speaker credentials that support revocation and unlinkability when protected biometric templates are exposed.
- Developed protected-domain speaker-verification pipelines using transformed speaker representations.
- Implemented CKKS-based encrypted similarity computation for privacy-preserving speaker verification.
- Investigate progressively encrypted verification configurations involving encrypted probes, enrollment references, and probe-to-reference matching.
- Evaluate encrypted verification using EER, AUC, FAR, FRR, threshold drift, score correlation, rank preservation, numerical error, runtime, memory use, and ciphertext-storage overhead.
- Examine residual privacy risks through membership-inference, sensitive-attribute-inference, and protected-representation leakage analysis.
- Investigate differential privacy as an additional protection mechanism while evaluating its effect on speaker discriminability and authentication utility.
- Published the resulting work on cancellable speaker credentials in **IEEE Access** in 2026.

**Research areas:** Privacy-preserving authentication, biometric-template protection, cancellable biometrics, homomorphic encryption, CKKS, privacy leakage, differential privacy, and secure artificial intelligence.

## Nara Institute of Science and Technology (NAIST)

### Graduate Research Assistant — Master’s Research

**October 2023 – September 2025**  
Ikoma, Nara, Japan

Developed secure voice-biometric systems for mobile banking, low-end devices, and real-world communication environments. The research addressed the effects of device limitations, microphone differences, background noise, communication channels, and acoustic degradation on speaker authentication.

- Built end-to-end speaker-identification and verification pipelines using machine learning, deep learning, and speech-processing methods.
- Used Whisper and ECAPA-TDNN representations to capture complementary speech and speaker information.
- Investigated preprocessing, speech enhancement, dimensionality reduction, classification, similarity scoring, and verification-threshold selection.
- Developed a secure speaker-identification system for mobile-banking users relying on low-end devices.
- Incorporated liveness detection to strengthen protection against authentication attempts involving non-live voice samples.
- Achieved 99.59% speaker-identification accuracy and approximately 99.60% F1-score in the evaluated system.
- Developed a drift-aware security framework for analysing verification reliability under progressively degraded acoustic conditions.
- Demonstrated how fixed verification thresholds can become unreliable when deployment conditions differ from the original evaluation environment.
- Created **SecureVoice50**, a multi-device and multi-environment dataset containing recordings from 50 speakers.
- Created a pilot corpus containing 480 recordings from 12 participants across multiple devices and indoor and outdoor environments.
- Produced three peer-reviewed IEEE conference papers, one master’s thesis, and two DOI-registered public research datasets.

**Research outputs:** IEEE APCC 2024, IEEE PCDS 2025, AAIML 2026, SecureVoice50, and the Pilot Multi-Device Speech Corpus.
